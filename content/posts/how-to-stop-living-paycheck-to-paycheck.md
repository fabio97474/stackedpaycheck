## Why Most People Are Stuck

Living paycheck to paycheck is not a sign of failure. It is a sign that nobody ever gave you a real system.

The standard advice — "spend less, save more" — is useless without a framework. This article gives you that framework.

## Step 1: Do a Money Audit (Day 1-3)

Open your last three bank statements. Categorize every transaction into:

- **Fixed essentials**: rent, utilities, insurance
- **Variable essentials**: groceries, transport
- **Discretionary**: subscriptions, dining out, shopping

Most people find $100-$300 in subscriptions and recurring charges they forgot about. Cancel anything unused.

## Step 2: Set Your Numbers (Day 4-7)

Use the 60/10/20/10 framework:

- **60%** for fixed and variable essentials
- **10%** for wants and discretionary spending
- **20%** for debt repayment or savings
- **10%** for your emergency buffer

Calculate what 60% of your take-home pay is. If your essentials cost more, look at what can be reduced — starting with housing and subscriptions.

## Step 3: Build Your First Buffer (Day 8-21)

Before tackling debt or investing, build a $500-$1,000 emergency buffer. This is not a full emergency fund — it is just enough to stop using credit cards when something unexpected happens.

Sell unused items. Pick up extra hours. Cut discretionary spending temporarily. Get to $500 as fast as possible.

## Step 4: Automate Everything (Day 22-30)

Set up automatic transfers on payday:

1. Transfer your savings/buffer amount immediately
2. Pay fixed bills automatically
3. Spend what remains on variables and discretionary

When money moves automatically, you cannot accidentally spend it.

## The Result

After 30 days you will have:
- A clear picture of where your money goes
- A working budget framework
- A small emergency buffer
- Automated savings

That is the foundation. Everything else — debt payoff, investing, building wealth — comes after this.
